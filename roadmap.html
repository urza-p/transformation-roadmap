Phase rationale

Crawl

Crawl groups the capabilities the later pilots need: connected factory data, central AI leadership, workforce mobility, and structured product environmental data. These can advance in parallel because none depends on another. The dates are real, so this is a retrospective foundation phase, not a claim that Schneider finished it in one short window.

Walk

Walk keeps deployment inside defined contexts: Copilot inside EcoStruxure Automation Expert and sustainability reporting inside EcoStruxure IT. The narrow scope lets the team check whether users come back, reports are accurate, and engineering work improves before a broader rollout. Measurable gates: repeated weekly adoption, verified time saved, report cycle time, and material error rates.

Run

Run scales what both pilots proved through the AI-Native Ecosystem and the Resource Advisor+ launch. This does not mean Resource Advisor+ and EcoStruxure IT are the same product; the ecosystem scales a connected set of capabilities. Scaling also needs non-software change: training, AI in advisory delivery, clear ownership of data quality and model oversight, and ongoing funding.

What each dependency passes forward
  • Smart Factory to Industrial Copilot: factory data and operating context for building and testing industrial AI.
  • AI Hub to Industrial Copilot: leadership and coordination for industrial AI.
  • Open Talent Market to Industrial Copilot: workforce readiness through role matching and development.
  • Smart Factory to Sustainability Reporting: operating data as a bounded test environment for automated reporting.
  • PEP Data to Sustainability Reporting: structured environmental data for reliable calculations.
  • Sustainability Reporting to AI-Native Ecosystem: automated reporting feeds connected sustainability workflows.
  • Industrial Copilot to AI-Native Ecosystem: industrial AI experience informs a broader AI-enabled service model.
