Phase rationale

Crawl

Crawl groups the capabilities that the later pilots need: connected factory information, central AI leadership, workforce mobility, and structured product environmental data. These foundations can advance in parallel because the roadmap does not place a dependency between one Crawl initiative and another. The two Walk initiatives then draw on different combinations of the foundation: Copilot uses AI coordination, factory context, and workforce readiness, while automated sustainability reporting needs usable factory and product data. This sequence follows capability dependency, while retaining the initiatives' actual dates to avoid implying the foundations appeared at once.

Walk

Walk confines deployment to defined contexts: Industrial Copilot inside the EcoStruxure Automation Expert Platform and sustainability reporting within the EcoStruxure IT product line. The narrower scope makes it possible to check whether users return, reports are accurate, and engineering work improves before the company commits to broader rollout. The group should use repeated weekly adoption, verified time saved, report-cycle time, and material error rates as measurable gates. These pilots produce evidence for Run investment and expose operating problems while the deployment remains bounded.

Run

Run scales capabilities demonstrated in both Walk initiatives through the AI-Native Ecosystem, whose Resource Advisor+ launch combines energy, emissions, supply-chain, and reporting workflows. The roadmap does not claim that Resource Advisor+ is the same product as EcoStruxure IT; it scales a connected set of capabilities. Broader deployment also requires changes beyond software: train users and service teams, embed AI-enabled workflows in advisory delivery, assign ongoing ownership for data quality and model oversight, and fund platform operations. Adoption and verified customer outcomes should remain scale gates.
